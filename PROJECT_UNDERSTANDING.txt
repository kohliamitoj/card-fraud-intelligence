================================================================================
  1. WHAT THIS PROJECT DOES (PLAIN ENGLISH)
================================================================================

  Imagine a bank receives thousands of card transactions every minute.
  Among these, a small number (around 2%) are fraudulent — someone using
  a stolen card, a criminal making an international wire transfer, or a
  cardholder's account being taken over.

  Traditionally, banks have two ways to catch fraud:
    a) Rule-based systems: "flag any transaction over ₹50,000 abroad"
       → Too many false alarms, misses clever fraud
    b) Human review teams: analysts manually check suspicious transactions
       → Too slow, too expensive, cannot scale

  This project builds a third approach: an AI + ML powered pipeline that:

    STEP 1: Receives a transaction the moment it happens
    STEP 2: Scores it with a machine learning model (0% to 100% fraud probability)
    STEP 3: Uses Google Gemini AI to write a plain-English explanation of WHY
            the model flagged it (e.g., "This ₹95,000 transfer to a Nigerian
            crypto exchange at 2am is 4x this cardholder's normal spend")
    STEP 4: Creates a "fraud case" in a dashboard for the analyst team
    STEP 5: Lets analysts investigate the case via an AI chat assistant,
            add notes, update the case status, and confirm or dismiss the flag

  The result: fraud analysts spend their time on the right cases, with AI
  doing the heavy lifting of pattern recognition and explanation.


================================================================================
  2. WHY IT MATTERS (BUSINESS CONTEXT)
================================================================================

  SCALE OF THE PROBLEM
  --------------------
  - India's card fraud losses exceeded ₹2,000 crore in FY2024
  - Globally, card fraud is a $33 billion annual problem
  - Every card-issuing bank (HDFC, SBI, ICICI, Axis, Kotak) runs a fraud
    operations team 24/7 to review flagged transactions

  REGULATORY CONTEXT
  ------------------
  - RBI mandates banks to file a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) under
    PMLA (Prevention of Money Laundering Act) for confirmed fraud above ₹10 lakh
  - Visa and Mastercard have chargeback reason codes that determine who bears
    the financial loss (issuing bank, acquiring bank, or merchant)
  - The AI chat assistant in this project specifically references these
    regulatory thresholds when suggesting next steps

  WHO USES THIS SYSTEM
  --------------------
  - Fraud Analyst: reviews flagged cases, adds investigation notes
  - Senior Analyst: can confirm fraud, close cases, approve chargebacks
  - Manager: monitors the analytics dashboard for portfolio-level risk

  WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT FROM A SIMPLE RULE ENGINE
  ----------------------------------------------------
  Rule engines flag based on single conditions ("amount > threshold").
  This ML model combines 21 different signals simultaneously:
    - How unusual is this amount compared to THIS cardholder's personal history?
    - How many transactions did this card make in the last hour?
    - Is this the first time this card has been used at this merchant?
    - Did the card physically swipe in Mumbai 4 minutes after swiping in Delhi
      (an impossible distance in that time)?
  Combining signals this way catches fraud that rules would miss entirely.

    - XGBoost (ML): Fast, deterministic, scores thousands of transactions

                    Returns a sentence. Called only when a transaction is flagged.


================================================================================
  4. DATA FLOW — END TO END
================================================================================

  Here is exactly what happens when a transaction is scored:

  STEP 1 — API RECEIVES TRANSACTION
    POST /api/v1/transactions/score
    Payload includes: amount, cardholder_id, merchant_id, MCC, channel,
    location, timestamp, card type

  STEP 2 — FETCH CARDHOLDER HISTORY
    The system fetches the last 30 days of this cardholder's transactions
    from MongoDB. This is needed to compute behavioral features like:
    "how unusual is this amount compared to their normal spending?"

  STEP 3 — FEATURE ENGINEERING (app/core/feature_builder.py)
    21 features are computed in real time from the transaction + history:
    (Full list in Section 5a)

  STEP 4 — ML SCORING (app/core/fraud_detector.py)
    The 21 features are passed to the loaded XGBoost model.
    Output: fraud_probability (e.g., 0.9312)
    The SHAP explainer simultaneously computes which features drove
    the score up or down (e.g., "is_high_risk_mcc: +0.21")

  STEP 5 — THRESHOLD CHECK
    If fraud_probability >= 0.5 (configurable): transaction is FLAGGED
    Risk level assigned:
      0.00 – 0.39 → LOW
      0.40 – 0.64 → MEDIUM
      0.65 – 0.84 → HIGH
      0.85 – 1.00 → CRITICAL

  STEP 6 — AI EXPLANATION (app/core/ai_assistant.py)
    If flagged, the SHAP values + transaction details are sent to Gemini.
    Gemini writes a 2-3 sentence explanation in plain English.
    Example output:
    "This ₹95,000 transfer to a crypto exchange in Lagos at 2am is 4x this
    cardholder's average spend. The merchant category (crypto/wire transfer)
    carries the highest fraud risk in our portfolio, and Nigeria is flagged
    as a high-risk country. Combined with the unusual hour, this pattern
    is consistent with account takeover fraud."

  STEP 7 — FRAUD CASE CREATED
    A new document is inserted into MongoDB "fraud_cases" collection with:
    - Unique case_id (UUID)
    - All transaction details
    - fraud_probability, risk_level
    - fraud_explanation (from Gemini)
    - top_risk_factors (from SHAP)
    - status: "OPEN"

  STEP 8 — RESPONSE RETURNED TO CALLER
    The API returns all of the above to whoever submitted the transaction
    (the frontend Live Demo page, or another banking system calling this API).

  STEP 9 — ANALYST INVESTIGATION (optional, human-driven)
    An analyst opens the case in the dashboard.
    They can:
      a) Read the AI explanation
      b) View the SHAP risk factor chart
      c) Chat with the AI assistant ("should I block this card?")
      d) Add investigation notes
      e) Update status (CONFIRMED_FRAUD / FALSE_POSITIVE / CLOSED)
    Every action is logged to the "audit_log" collection.


================================================================================
  5. MACHINE LEARNING LAYER (XGBoost + SHAP)
================================================================================

  5a. FEATURES ENGINEERED (21 total)
  ------------------------------------
  These 21 features are computed for every transaction, both during
  training (offline) and during scoring (real time). It is critical that
  both use identical logic — a bug here causes "training-serving skew"
  which silently breaks model accuracy.

  The training version is in: training/feature_engineering.py
  The serving version is in:  app/core/feature_builder.py

  AMOUNT FEATURES:
    amount                    — Raw transaction amount in INR
    amount_zscore             — (amount - cardholder_avg) / cardholder_std
                                How many standard deviations above their
                                normal spending is this transaction?
                                zscore > 3 means extremely unusual.

  TIME FEATURES:
    hour_of_day               — 0 to 23. Fraud peaks 1am–5am.
    day_of_week               — 0 (Mon) to 6 (Sun).
    is_night                  — 1 if hour < 6 or hour >= 22, else 0.
    is_weekend                — 1 if Saturday or Sunday, else 0.

  VELOCITY FEATURES:
    velocity_1h               — Number of transactions by this cardholder
                                in the past 1 hour before this transaction.
                                Fraudsters often make many small test
                                transactions before a large one.
    velocity_24h              — Same for past 24 hours.
    velocity_7d               — Same for past 7 days.
    amount_velocity_1h        — Total amount spent in the past 1 hour.
    amount_velocity_24h       — Total amount spent in the past 24 hours.

  TIME DELTA FEATURES:
    time_since_last_txn_seconds — Seconds since the previous transaction
                                  by this cardholder. Very short gaps
                                  combined with high amounts = suspicious.

  LOCATION FEATURES:
    distance_from_last_txn_km — Haversine distance (km) between this
                                 transaction's location and the previous
                                 one. Computed from lat/lng.
    impossible_travel          — 1 if speed between two consecutive
                                  locations exceeds 900 km/h (faster than
                                  any commercial flight). Catches cases
                                  where a stolen card is used simultaneously
                                  in two different cities.
    is_international           — 1 if location_country != "IN".
    is_new_country             — 1 if cardholder has never transacted in
                                  this country before (in 30-day history).
    is_high_risk_country       — 1 if country is in a hardcoded high-risk
                                  list: NG (Nigeria), RO (Romania), BR
                                  (Brazil), UA (Ukraine), PK (Pakistan).

  MERCHANT FEATURES:
    is_new_merchant            — 1 if cardholder has never transacted at
                                  this merchant_id before.
    is_high_risk_mcc           — 1 if the Merchant Category Code is in
                                  the high-risk list:
                                  7995 (Gambling), 6051 (Crypto/Wire),
                                  4829 (Money Transfer), 5912 (Pharmacy),
                                  5999 (Misc Retail), 6050, 7273.

  CHANNEL FEATURES:
    is_online                  — 1 if channel == "ONLINE" (card-not-present
                                  transactions are higher risk).
    is_atm                     — 1 if channel == "ATM".


  5b. HOW THE MODEL IS TRAINED
  -----------------------------
  Algorithm: XGBoost (eXtreme Gradient Boosting)
  Why XGBoost and not a neural network?
    - Tabular data: XGBoost consistently outperforms neural nets on
      structured/tabular data like transaction records.
    - Speed: XGBoost scores a transaction in microseconds. A neural net
      would be 10-100x slower with no accuracy gain here.
    - Explainability: XGBoost integrates perfectly with SHAP, which is
      critical for telling analysts WHY a transaction was flagged.
    - Industry standard: Major banks (JPMorgan, HDFC, Paytm) use
      gradient boosting for real-time fraud scoring.

  Training procedure:
    1. Load IEEE-CIS Fraud Detection dataset (590k real transactions)
       OR generate synthetic data (50k transactions, 2% fraud rate)
    2. Engineer all 21 features (offline, on full dataset)
    3. Apply SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) to handle
       class imbalance — explained in 5c
    4. Train XGBoost with 5-fold stratified cross-validation
       (5 folds = model is tested on 5 different validation splits,
        final score is averaged — more reliable than a single split)
    5. Evaluate on AUC-ROC and Average Precision
    6. Train final model on 100% of the data
    7. Build SHAP TreeExplainer on the final model
    8. Save model.pkl and shap_explainer.pkl

  Key XGBoost hyperparameters used:
    n_estimators=500       — 500 decision trees in the ensemble
    max_depth=7            — Each tree can go 7 levels deep
    learning_rate=0.05     — Small steps prevent overfitting
    subsample=0.8          — Each tree uses 80% of training rows (random)
    colsample_bytree=0.5   — Each tree uses 50% of features (random)
    scale_pos_weight       — Automatically set to (n_legit / n_fraud)
                             to give more weight to the rare fraud class


  5c. HOW CLASS IMBALANCE IS HANDLED
  ------------------------------------
  The fundamental problem: only ~2% of transactions are fraud.
  If the model predicts "not fraud" for everything, it is 98% accurate
  but catches ZERO fraud cases. Accuracy is a useless metric here.

  TWO techniques are used:

  SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique):
    Creates synthetic fraud examples by interpolating between real ones.
    sampling_strategy=0.15 means: make fraud = 15% of the training data.
    This gives the model more fraud examples to learn from.

  scale_pos_weight (XGBoost parameter):
    Makes the model penalize missing a fraud case much more than
    falsely flagging a legitimate transaction.
    Formula: scale_pos_weight = n_legitimate / n_fraud
    Example: if 50,000 legit and 1,000 fraud → scale_pos_weight = 50

  Correct evaluation metrics:
    AUC-ROC: Area Under the ROC Curve. Measures how well the model
             separates fraud from legitimate at all thresholds.
             1.0 = perfect, 0.5 = random. Target: > 0.95.
    Average Precision: Average of precision values at all recall thresholds.
             Better than AUC-ROC when classes are highly imbalanced.
             Target: > 0.80.


  5d. HOW SHAP WORKS HERE
  ------------------------
  SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) answers the question:
  "For THIS specific transaction, how much did each feature contribute
  to pushing the score up or down?"

  Example SHAP output for a flagged transaction:
    is_high_risk_mcc      : +0.21  (increased fraud probability by 0.21)
    amount_zscore         : +0.18  (amount is 4x normal → very suspicious)
    is_high_risk_country  : +0.16  (Nigeria in the high-risk list)
    velocity_1h           : +0.12  (3 transactions in the last hour)
    is_night              : +0.10  (2am transaction)
    is_new_merchant       : +0.09  (first time at this merchant)
    is_weekend            : -0.03  (slightly less suspicious on a weekend)
    is_online             : +0.08  (card-not-present increases risk)

  These SHAP values are:
  1. Displayed as a bar chart in the Case Detail page (frontend)
  2. Sent to Gemini AI to generate the human-readable explanation
     (Gemini translates "+0.21 from is_high_risk_mcc" into
      "the merchant category (crypto/wire transfer) carries the
       highest fraud risk in our portfolio")

  The SHAP TreeExplainer is pre-built on the training data and saved
  as shap_explainer.pkl so it can be loaded at API startup without
  retraining.


================================================================================
  6. AI LAYER (GOOGLE GEMINI)
================================================================================

  Gemini is used in three distinct ways. In all three cases, the LLM
  is given structured context (transaction data, SHAP values, case history)
  and asked to produce a specific type of output.

  Model used: gemini-2.0-flash
  Why Flash (not Pro/Ultra)? Speed and cost.
    - Fraud explanation needs to happen in near real-time
    - Flash gives good quality for structured explanation tasks
    - Pro would be overkill and 5-10x more expensive

  6a. FRAUD EXPLANATION
  ----------------------
  Called: When a transaction is flagged (fraud_probability >= threshold)
  Input to Gemini:
    - Transaction details (amount, merchant, MCC, channel, country, time)
    - fraud_probability (e.g., "93.1%")
    - Top SHAP risk factors with impact values and directions
  Output: 2-3 sentence professional explanation
  Tone instruction: "Write as a senior fraud analyst. Use plain language.
                     Do not mention SHAP or ML terms."

  Example:
    Input SHAP factors:
      is_high_risk_country: +0.24
      is_high_risk_mcc:     +0.21
      amount_zscore:        +0.18
      is_night:             +0.12

    Output Gemini explanation:
      "This transaction is highly suspicious: a ₹95,000 transfer to a
      crypto exchange in Lagos at 2am is far outside this cardholder's
      typical spending pattern. The merchant category (crypto/wire
      transfer) and the destination country (Nigeria) are both associated
      with elevated fraud risk in our portfolio. This combination is
      consistent with an account takeover scenario."

  Fallback: If GEMINI_API_KEY is not set or the API call fails, the
  system generates a simpler template-based explanation from the SHAP
  values directly. The API never fails because of a missing Gemini key.


  6b. INVESTIGATION CHAT
  -----------------------
  Called: When an analyst sends a message in the Case Detail page chat
  Input to Gemini:
    - Full case context (amount, merchant, MCC, fraud probability,
      risk level, current status, all analyst notes so far)
    - Last 6 messages of conversation history (sliding window)
    - Analyst's current question
  Output:
    - Answer to the question with banking domain context
    - List of suggested next actions (e.g., "Block card", "File STR")

  Gemini is instructed to reference real banking procedures:
    - CBR (Chargeback Request) if merchant dispute is applicable
    - STR filing if amount exceeds ₹10 lakhs (PMLA requirement)
    - Card blocking procedures
    - Cardholder notification steps

  Example chat exchange:
    Analyst: "Should I block the card immediately?"
    Gemini:  "Yes, given the 93% fraud probability and CRITICAL risk
              level, immediate card blocking is recommended. Issue a
              replacement card to the cardholder. If the cardholder
              confirms they did not authorize this transaction, initiate
              a chargeback request. Since the amount exceeds ₹10 lakhs,
              a Suspicious Transaction Report must be filed with FIU-IND
              within 7 working days under PMLA."

  Suggested actions (auto-generated based on risk level):
    CRITICAL (>85%): Block card · Initiate CBR · File STR · Notify cardholder
    HIGH (65-85%):   Temporary hold · Review 30-day history


  6c. CASE SUMMARY
  -----------------
  Called: When analyst opens Case Detail page (generated once, cached)
  Input to Gemini:
    - All case fields
    - Up to 3 similar historical cases (found by MongoDB query matching
      on MCC, cardholder_id, or risk_level)
  Output:
    - Executive summary (3-4 sentences covering what happened,
      why suspicious, and recommended action)
    - Bullet-point list of red flags
    - Single recommended action sentence

  Once generated, the summary is stored in the fraud_cases document
  (ai_summary field) so subsequent visits don't re-call Gemini.


================================================================================
  7. BACKEND API (FastAPI)
================================================================================

  Framework: FastAPI (Python)
  Why FastAPI?
    - Async by design: handles many concurrent transaction scoring
      requests without blocking
    - Auto-generates Swagger UI at /docs (great for portfolio demos)
    - Pydantic v2 validation: wrong input format is caught automatically
      before it reaches business logic
    - Motor (async MongoDB driver) pairs naturally with FastAPI's async

  Port: 8000 (local) / Railway-assigned PORT (production)

  7a. AUTHENTICATION
  -------------------
  Mechanism: JWT (JSON Web Tokens) with HMAC-SHA256 signing
  Library: python-jose

  Flow:
    1. POST /api/v1/auth/login with {username, password}
    2. Server verifies password against bcrypt hash in MongoDB
    3. Server issues a JWT containing {sub: username, role: role, exp: expiry}
    4. Client stores JWT in localStorage
    5. All subsequent requests include: Authorization: Bearer <token>
    6. FastAPI dependency (require_auth) decodes and validates the token

  Password storage: bcrypt hash (passlib) — never stored in plain text

  Roles:
    analyst        — can score transactions, view cases, add notes
    senior_analyst — can also update case status
    manager        — full access including analytics

  Token expiry: 60 minutes (configurable in .env)

  7b. TRANSACTION SCORING ENDPOINT
  ----------------------------------
  POST /api/v1/transactions/score

  This is the most important endpoint. Here is exactly what it does:

    1. Validates the request body with Pydantic (all required fields,
       correct types, valid enum values for card_type/channel)
    2. Calls _fetch_cardholder_history(): queries MongoDB for last 30
       days of this cardholder's transactions (max 200 records)
    3. Calls _compute_cardholder_stats(): MongoDB aggregation to get
       avg_amount and std_amount for amount z-score calculation
    4. Calls build_features(): computes all 21 features in real time
    5. Calls score_transaction(): passes features to XGBoost model,
       gets fraud_probability and SHAP values
    6. Calls risk_level_from_prob(): maps probability to LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH/CRITICAL
    7. If is_flagged: calls generate_fraud_explanation() (Gemini API)
       and creates a fraud case document in MongoDB
    8. Stores the transaction in MongoDB "transactions" collection
    9. Returns TransactionScoreResponse with all results

  Response time target: < 200ms
  Bottleneck: MongoDB history query (solved by indexing cardholder_id + timestamp)

  7c. FRAUD CASE LIFECYCLE
  -------------------------
  A fraud case goes through these statuses:

    OPEN               — Just created when transaction flagged
        ↓
    UNDER_INVESTIGATION — Analyst has started reviewing
        ↓
    CONFIRMED_FRAUD    — Analyst confirmed it is real fraud
        or
    FALSE_POSITIVE     — Analyst determined it was a legitimate transaction
        ↓
    CLOSED             — Case resolved and documented

  Every status change is logged to the audit_log collection with:
    - who made the change (analyst username)
    - what the previous status was
    - the reason given
    - timestamp

  Endpoints for case management:
    GET    /api/v1/cases/               — list all cases (filterable)
    GET    /api/v1/cases/{id}           — full case detail
    PATCH  /api/v1/cases/{id}/status   — update status
    POST   /api/v1/cases/{id}/notes    — add investigation note
    PATCH  /api/v1/cases/{id}/assign   — assign to an analyst

  7d. ANALYTICS ENDPOINTS
  ------------------------
  GET /api/v1/analytics/dashboard
    Returns aggregate KPIs using MongoDB $group aggregations:
    - total_transactions_scored
    - total_flagged, flag_rate_percent
    - total_cases, open_cases, critical_cases
    - confirmed_fraud_cases, false_positive_cases
    - detection_rate_percent, false_positive_rate_percent
    - total_confirmed_fraud_amount_inr

  GET /api/v1/analytics/trends?days=30
    Daily breakdown of cases for the past N days.
    Uses MongoDB $dateToString and $group to aggregate by date.

  GET /api/v1/analytics/by-merchant-category
    Top 15 MCCs by flagged transaction count.
    Shows which merchant categories generate most fraud.

  GET /api/v1/analytics/by-channel
    Breakdown by POS / ONLINE / ATM / CONTACTLESS.
    Shows which channel has highest fraud rate.


================================================================================
  8. DATABASE DESIGN (MongoDB)
================================================================================

  Why MongoDB instead of PostgreSQL?
    - Transaction records are semi-structured (different transactions
      may have different optional fields like latitude, ip_address)
    - Case documents embed notes as an array — no need for a separate
      notes table with joins
    - Fast writes: MongoDB is optimized for high-throughput inserts,
      important for real-time transaction logging
    - Flexible schema: adding new features to the transaction document
      doesn't require an ALTER TABLE migration

  Database: card_fraud_intelligence

  COLLECTION 1: transactions
  --------------------------
  One document per transaction scored. Stores everything:
    {
      transaction_id, cardholder_id, card_last4, card_type,
      amount, currency,
      merchant_id, merchant_name, merchant_category_code,
      channel, location_city, location_country, latitude, longitude,
      ip_address, device_fingerprint, timestamp,
      fraud_probability, is_flagged, risk_level,
      fraud_explanation, top_risk_factors,
      case_id (if flagged, links to fraud_cases),
      created_at
    }
  Indexes recommended:
    - (cardholder_id, timestamp): for fast history lookup during scoring
    - (is_flagged): for filtering flagged transactions
    - (transaction_id): unique index for deduplication

  COLLECTION 2: fraud_cases
  -------------------------
  One document per flagged transaction case:
    {
      case_id (UUID),
      transaction_id (links to transactions),
      cardholder_id, amount, currency,
      merchant_name, merchant_category_code,
      fraud_probability, risk_level,
      fraud_explanation (from Gemini),
      top_risk_factors (SHAP values list),
      status (OPEN/UNDER_INVESTIGATION/CONFIRMED_FRAUD/FALSE_POSITIVE/CLOSED),
      assigned_to (analyst username),
      notes: [                        ← embedded array
        { note_id, analyst, content, created_at },
        ...
      ],
      ai_summary (cached Gemini summary),
      created_at, updated_at
    }

  COLLECTION 3: users
  -------------------
  One document per analyst account:
    {
      username, email, full_name, role,
      hashed_password (bcrypt),
      is_active, created_at
    }

  COLLECTION 4: audit_log
  -----------------------
  Immutable log of all analyst actions:
    {
      event (case_status_updated / investigation_query / ...),
      case_id, analyst,
      from_status, to_status, reason,
      question (for chat events),
      timestamp
    }
  This collection is append-only — nothing is ever updated or deleted.
  Important for compliance and dispute resolution.

  HOW VERCEL HANDLES ROUTING:
    React Router uses client-side routing. If someone visits
    yourdomain.vercel.app/cases/abc123 directly, Vercel needs to serve
    index.html (not a 404). The vercel.json file handles this:
    { "rewrites": [{ "source": "/(.*)", "destination": "/index.html" }] }

  HOW RAILWAY GETS THE API KEY / SECRETS:
    Never committed to Git. Set in Railway dashboard as Environment
    Variables. Railway injects them as process environment variables
    at container startup. FastAPI reads them via pydantic-settings
    from the .env file or environment.

  HOW THE MODEL FILES ARE HANDLED:
    .pkl files are large (50-200MB) and are in .gitignore.
    For production, options are:
    a) Mount them via Railway's persistent volume storage
    b) Download from Google Cloud Storage at container startup
    c) Use demo mode (API works without model, returns realistic scores)
    The API gracefully handles a missing model with a warning log
    and falls back to demo scoring.

  AUTO-DEPLOY FLOW:
    Developer pushes to GitHub main branch
      → Railway detects change → rebuilds Docker image → redeploys (3-5 min)
      → Vercel detects change → rebuilds React bundle → redeploys (1-2 min)
    Zero-downtime: Railway keeps old container running until new one is ready


================================================================================
  11. KEY DESIGN DECISIONS AND WHY
================================================================================

  DECISION 1: MongoDB over PostgreSQL
  Reason: Fraud cases have an embedded notes array that can grow
  dynamically. In SQL this would be a separate table requiring JOINs.
  In MongoDB it's a simple array in the document. Also, the flexible
  schema makes it easy to add new features to transactions without
  migrations.

  DECISION 2: Sync ML scoring, async Gemini
  The XGBoost model scores in < 1ms. The Gemini API call takes 1-3
  seconds. Both are awaited in the transaction scoring endpoint. This
  means the scoring endpoint takes 1-3 seconds when a transaction is
  flagged. For a portfolio project this is acceptable. In production,
  the Gemini call would be moved to a background task (FastAPI
  BackgroundTasks) so the API returns the score instantly and the
  explanation is generated asynchronously.

  DECISION 3: SHAP over other explainability methods
  LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) is an alternative
  but is slower and less consistent for tree models. SHAP TreeExplainer
  is an exact algorithm specifically for tree ensembles — computationally
  efficient and mathematically guaranteed to be additive (all SHAP values
  sum to the model output).

  DECISION 4: 5-fold cross validation over train/test split
  A single 80/20 split can be misleading with only 1,000 fraud samples.
  5-fold CV trains and evaluates 5 times on different data splits, giving
  a reliable estimate of how the model will perform on unseen data.

  DECISION 5: React Context over Redux for state management
  The only truly global state is auth (user + token). Everything else
  is page-local. Using Redux for this would be massive over-engineering.
  React Context + localStorage is sufficient and simpler.

  DECISION 6: Motor (async MongoDB) over PyMongo (sync)
  FastAPI is async. If we used sync PyMongo, every database call would
  block the async event loop, defeating the purpose of FastAPI's
  concurrency. Motor is the official async MongoDB driver.

  DECISION 7: Tailwind CSS over styled-components or CSS modules
  Utility-first CSS means the design stays consistent without writing
  custom CSS. Custom CSS is only added for the gauge animation and
  fade-in effect in index.css. Tailwind's purge removes unused classes
  so the production bundle is small.

  12. WHAT EACH FILE DOES

  BACKEND FILES
  -------------
  config/settings.py
    Reads all environment variables (.env file or system env) using
    pydantic-settings. Every other module imports from here.
    Never hardcodes secrets.

  app/db/mongodb.py
    Manages the MongoDB connection lifecycle.
    connect_db(): called at API startup (lifespan event)
    close_db(): called at API shutdown
    get_collection(): returns a collection by name for use in services

  app/schemas/
    Pydantic models for request/response validation.
    transaction.py: TransactionRequest (what comes IN), TransactionScoreResponse (what goes OUT)
    case.py: CaseResponse, CaseStatusUpdate, AddNoteRequest, InvestigationNote
    user.py: UserCreate, UserInDB, TokenResponse, LoginRequest
    investigation.py: ChatMessage, InvestigationChatRequest, CaseSummaryResponse

  app/core/feature_builder.py
    The real-time feature engineering. Builds the 21 features from a
    transaction dict + history list. Must stay in sync with
    training/feature_engineering.py.
    Also contains FEATURE_NAMES list (used to ensure feature order matches
    what the model was trained on).

  app/core/fraud_detector.py
    Loads model and SHAP explainer from .pkl files at startup.
    score_transaction(): takes 21 features, returns (probability, shap_values)
    risk_level_from_prob(): maps float to LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH/CRITICAL string
    Falls back to demo mode if model files are missing.

  app/core/ai_assistant.py
    All Gemini API calls. Three functions:
    generate_fraud_explanation(): takes SHAP + txn → 2-3 sentence explanation
    answer_investigation_query(): takes case + question → answer + suggested actions
    generate_case_summary(): takes case + similar cases → executive summary

  app/services/transaction_service.py
    Orchestrates the full scoring flow. Calls feature_builder, fraud_detector,
    ai_assistant in sequence. Stores results to MongoDB.

  app/services/case_service.py
    CRUD for fraud cases. Status updates, note additions, assignments.
    All writes also create audit_log entries.

  app/services/investigation_service.py
    Chat and summary logic. Finds similar cases via MongoDB query.
    Caches AI summaries in the case document.

  app/services/auth_service.py
    User registration, login, password hashing (bcrypt), JWT token
    creation and decoding.

  app/api/v1/endpoints/
    Thin route handlers. Validate input via Pydantic, call services,
    return responses. No business logic here.

  app/dependencies.py
    FastAPI dependency injection functions.
    require_auth: extracts and validates JWT from Authorization header
    require_senior: checks role is senior_analyst or manager

  app/main.py
    FastAPI application setup. Lifespan function handles startup
    (connect DB, load model, init Gemini) and shutdown (close DB).
    CORS middleware allows the React frontend to call the API.

  TRAINING FILES

  training/generate_synthetic_data.py

  13. GLOSSARY OF BANKING TERMS USED
================================================================================

  MCC (Merchant Category Code)
    A 4-digit code assigned to every merchant by the card networks
    (Visa, Mastercard). Examples:
      5411 = Grocery Stores
      5812 = Eating Places and Restaurants
      7995 = Gambling / Betting
      6051 = Non-Financial Institutions — Foreign Currency / Money Orders
             (this is what crypto exchanges use)
      4829 = Money Transfers
    Fraudsters favour high-risk MCCs because they move money quickly
    and are harder to reverse.

  Card-Not-Present (CNP) Fraud
    Fraud where the physical card is not used. Online transactions are
    CNP. Higher risk because the merchant cannot verify the cardholder
    is physically present. The ONLINE channel in this project maps to CNP.

  Chargeback (CBR)
    The process of reversing a fraudulent charge. The cardholder disputes
    the transaction with their bank (issuer). The issuer claims the money
    back from the merchant's bank (acquirer). Visa/Mastercard reason codes
    determine who bears the loss.

  STR (Suspicious Transaction Report)
    A mandatory report filed with FIU-IND (Financial Intelligence Unit
    — India) under PMLA (Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002).
    Required for: confirmed fraud above ₹10 lakhs, or any transaction
    suggesting money laundering. Must be filed within 7 working days.

  AUC-ROC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve)
    A measure of a fraud model's ability to distinguish fraud from
    legitimate transactions. 1.0 = perfect. 0.5 = random (useless).
    Typical production fraud models: 0.92 - 0.98.

  False Positive
    A legitimate transaction incorrectly flagged as fraud.
    Too many false positives: good customers are frustrated when their
    cards are declined. Banks aim to keep false positive rate < 5%.

  False Negative
    A fraudulent transaction that was NOT flagged (missed fraud).
    Each false negative = direct financial loss to the bank or cardholder.

  PMLA
    Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002. Indian law requiring banks
    to file STRs and maintain transaction records for 5 years.

  FIU-IND
    Financial Intelligence Unit — India. The government body that
    receives STRs from banks and investigates financial crimes.

  Velocity (in fraud context)
    The number of transactions made by a card in a short time window.
    High velocity (e.g., 5 transactions in 10 minutes) is a major
    fraud signal — fraudsters test cards with small amounts before
    making large unauthorized purchases.

  Skimming
    Physical theft of card data by placing a device on ATMs or POS
    terminals. The stolen data is used to make fraudulent card-present
    transactions, often in a different city or country.

  Account Takeover (ATO)
    A fraudster gains access to a legitimate cardholder's online
    account (via phishing or credential stuffing) and makes purchases.
    Patterns: login from new device/IP, followed by new payee added,
    followed by large transfer. The "account_takeover" fraud type in
    the synthetic data generator models this pattern.
